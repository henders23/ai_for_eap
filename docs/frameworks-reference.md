## UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Teachers (AI CFT)

Two-dimensional matrix: **5 aspects × 3 progression levels = 15 competency blocks** (Table 1, p.22).

Progression levels:
- **Acquire** — essential competencies all teachers need to evaluate, select and use AI tools appropriately ("teachers' AI literacy").
- **Deepen** — intermediate competencies to design meaningful pedagogical strategies that integrate AI.
- **Create** — advanced competencies for creative configuration and innovative use of AI in education.

| # | Aspect | Acquire | Deepen | Create |
|---|--------|---------|--------|--------|
| 1 | Human-centred mindset | Human agency | Human accountability | Social responsibility |
| 2 | Ethics of AI | Ethical principles | Safe and responsible use | Co-creating ethical rules |
| 3 | AI foundations and applications | Basic AI techniques and applications | Application skills | Creating with AI |
| 4 | AI pedagogy | AI-assisted teaching | AI–pedagogy integration | AI-enhanced pedagogical transformation |
| 5 | AI for professional development | AI enabling lifelong professional learning | AI to enhance organizational learning | AI to support professional transformation |

Aspect definitions (Ch.3.2):
1. **Human-centred mindset** — values/critical attitudes toward human–AI interaction; puts human rights and human flourishing at the focus; ensures human agency and accountability; understands AI's societal impact.
2. **Ethics of AI** — essential ethical values, principles, regulations, institutional laws and practical ethical rules teachers understand and apply; progressing to participating in adapting ethical rules.
3. **AI foundations and applications** — conceptual knowledge + transferable operational skills to understand, select, apply and creatively customize AI tools for student-centred, AI-assisted environments.
4. **AI pedagogy** — competencies for purposeful, effective AI–pedagogy integration (course prep, teaching, learning, socialization, assessment); critically assessing when/how to use AI.
5. **AI for professional development** — using AI to drive lifelong professional learning, collaborative PD, and professional transformation.

Citation: UNESCO. (2024). *AI competency framework for teachers.* UNESCO. (Openly licensed, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO.)

## BALEAP TEAP Individual Accreditation Criteria (2022 revision)

Grouped originally (2008 framework, Fig.1) into: Professional Development; Research & Scholarship;
Programme Development; Academic Practices; The Student; Course Delivery (+2014 optional mentor/assessor unit).

**Current (2022) criteria** = **four core values** + **four broad areas of practice**, across **three pathways**
(Associate Fellow, Fellow, Senior Fellow).

### Four core values (must be evidenced throughout an application)
- **V1** — You are committed to evidence-based practice within your professional context. *(Professionalism)*
- **V2** — You value research, scholarship, and selective use of resources (such as technology) to enhance developmental activities. *(Development)*
- **V3** — You value collaboration for learning and professional practice. *(Collaboration)*
- **V4** — You respect individual learners' expectations and values, encourage participation in higher education, and promote equality of opportunity. *(Inclusivity)*

### Four areas of practice
1. **Planning & Design**
2. **Teaching & Learning**
3. **Assessment & Feedback**
4. **Scholarship & Development**

Each area has KNOWLEDGE and ACTIVITIES statements, coded per pathway. Associate Fellow (entry) statements
are coded AP/AT/AA/AS (P=planning, T=teaching, A=assessment, S=scholarship); Fellow = FT-series; Senior = ST-series.

### Associate Fellow — verbatim statements (entry pathway)

**Planning & Design**
- Knowledge: AP1 characteristics of academic texts (rhetorical/discourse features beyond sentence level); AP2 academic & professional attributes (critical thinking, autonomy); AP3 strategies for processing/producing texts (oral or written).
- Activities: AP4 incorporate textual features & processing strategies into lesson planning; AP5 select and adapt appropriate EAP materials and modes of delivery; AP6 consider, anticipate and check student needs.

**Teaching & Learning**
- AT1 articulate personal principles of EAP teaching and learning; AT2 articulate examples of student-centred EAP teaching techniques + rationales; AT3 recognise a range of EAP syllabus/course types; AT4 adapt material for specific academic contexts/disciplines; AT5 teaching develops academic & professional attributes (critical thinking, autonomy); AT6 stage and scaffold teaching of academic communication; AT7 integrate study skills & strategies explicitly; AT8 develop students' ability to navigate conventions/values of current/future educational contexts; AT9 use classroom assessment techniques to demonstrate learning & inform future teaching; AT10 raise awareness of discourse features; AT11 train students to investigate the practices of a discipline; AT12 respond flexibly to unplanned learning opportunities; AT13 encourage a critical approach to knowledge building.

**Assessment & Feedback**
- AA1 articulate the difference between assessment of, as, and for learning; AA2 recognise principles of effective assessment & feedback delivery (marking criteria, consistency); AA3 articulate the principle of constructive alignment.

**Scholarship & Development**
- AS1 articulate approaches for gathering data to develop your teaching practice.

(Fellow = FT1–FT9, Senior Fellow = ST1–ST9 mirror the same four areas at higher impact/breadth.)

Underlying framework: BALEAP. (2008). *Competency Framework for Teachers of English for Academic Purposes.*
